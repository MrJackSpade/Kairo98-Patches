The public library is planning a small community garden beside its main entrance. The space is sunny in the morning and shaded by nearby trees after lunch. Volunteers have offered to build raised beds, but the staff must decide how to share the work fairly and keep the garden useful through the summer. The budget is modest, so the first season will focus on herbs, tomatoes, and flowers that attract pollinators. A rain barrel could reduce water use, although someone would still need to check the soil during dry weeks. The library also hopes to post simple signs explaining what grows in each bed and invite children to record changes they notice.

Write a practical, friendly plan for the garden's first three months. Include how volunteers should divide the work, how the library can involve visitors, and one way to evaluate whether the project is working.
